Models, sounds, fonts

Files of your own, in the game. Blender exports, Ogg tunes, TrueType faces - and the game's own textures swapped for a PNG.

A model from Blender (glTF)

  1. Export from Blender as glTF Binary (.glb). Units: the game's - a villager is about 10 tall, a doorway about 3 wide. +Y is up, +Z is forward. Textures embedded, or a .gltf with its files beside it.
  2. Import: the model picker's Import a model… (anywhere a model is picked), or copy the file into the project's assets\ folder.
  3. Use it: as an object's Model on a map (a prop, a building, a whole ground with Solid on), as a weapon's look, or as a character with Roam - the fox.
  4. Animate it: Blender actions come along as clips. A Mesh's Clip loops one; Roam plays Walk and Idle clips; a weapon's Clip loops while held; a timeline's glTF clip switches them in a cutscene.
What carries over: geometry, normals, texture coordinates, textures, vertex colours, node transforms, skins and animations. Lighting is the game's - a shade from the normal, as its own models have - so do not bake dramatic lighting into the textures.

A character remade: a high-res Luneth

The game's characters and monsters can be given a new mesh while their skeleton and every battle motion stay the game's. Works on the Steam game too - the result is the game's own format.

  1. Open the model (FF3 ▸ Models ▸ j101 is the first character) and Export .glb - + all motions to check your work against every swing in Blender.
  2. In Blender, model over it. Keep the armature as it came; weight your mesh to its bones (the vertex groups already carry the game's names: hara, mune, L_ude, R_te…). Do not move or rename bones. Export a .glb with the skin.
  3. Back in the viewer, with a project open: Remake from glTF…, pick your file (or Import a model… right there). The model reloads as yours; the transport plays the game's motions on it. Undo remake puts the game's back.

No rig? Skip step 2. A plain mesh, standing with its arms down, is bound to the character's skeleton for you when you Remake with it: scaled to the character, stood on its feet, weighted from the original's own skinning. The rigged copy appears in assets\ as <name>-rigged.glb - open it under Models and the transport plays every game motion on it, so you can orbit and scrub for a wrong weight before it ever goes in the game. The closer the mesh's shape to the original character's, the better.

On OpenFF the client draws your glTF directly - smooth weights as painted, any triangle count, any textures - through the game's skeleton and motions (Remake writes defs/models/j101.json for it). On Steam the game's own format has its rules: a vertex goes through one bone, or one of the blends the original had (a few at the knees, elbows and hips) - smooth weights snap to the nearest and the status line says how many; 10,922 triangles per model; textures 512 KB per model. Either way the bones must keep the original's names, and a mesh with no weights is bound to the nearest bone.

A sound or a tune

  1. FF3 ▸ Audio ▸ Import a sound…. An Ogg Vorbis or WAV file.
  2. Pick music (a BGM number the game does not use, 59 and up) or effect (an archive of your own, SE300_00 and up).
  3. A Music behaviour's Bgm and a Sound's Archive / Number list it; so do the timeline's music and sound clips and C#'s Game.Audio.

A font

  1. FF3 ▸ Text ▸ Import a font…. A .ttf or .otf.
  2. The client draws every line of text from it; glyphs it lacks fall back to the game's faces. The preview shows sample text at the game's sizes.

Pictures and textures

  1. FF3 ▸ Images: the menus' pictures. Open one, Replace… with a PNG of the same size. Import a PNG… adds a new one under a name of your own for the menus to reference.
  2. FF3 ▸ Textures: a model's skin. Open a texture package, pick a texture, Replace with a PNG - the game's formats and palettes are made for you. A recoloured villager is this and nothing else.
  3. Duplicate as… on any model copies it (and its textures) under a new name, so a recolour can live beside the original.

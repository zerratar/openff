Two programs

OpenFF plays the games. It reads your Steam copy of Final Fantasy III or IV and runs it with a modern client: any window size, game pads, and a mods folder that can add new maps, items, monsters, cutscenes and C# code.

Crystal is the editor. It opens the game's files - maps, models, text, tables, sound - and lets you change them or add your own. Everything you make is a project; Run in OpenFF plays it.

Getting startedInstall, open Crystal, open a sample project, play it. 5 minutes. The map editorSelect, move, add an object, give it a behaviour: a talking villager, a chest. A map of your ownA new map from a ground model, exits to and from the game's maps, music. Items and weaponsA new item from an existing one; a sword drawn from your own glTF, fitted in the hand. CutscenesCamera moves, lines, a fox that runs over: the timeline. Models, sounds, fontsBring a Blender export, an Ogg tune, a TrueType font into the game. C# codeWhen a behaviour is not enough: a script that hot-reloads while the game runs. Steam modsEdits that install into the Steam game itself - texts, textures, tables. ReferenceEvery component and its fields, every timeline clip, the files, the settings.

The game's own files, in the editor

Most of Crystal is about the game as shipped: its maps with their characters and exits in 3D, every line of text in every language, the tables behind items and monsters, the models with their animations, textures, menus, sound. Open, change, save - into your project, never into the game.
